## 📖 MY STORY & JOURNEY

Hey, I'm Michael Lannen. I grew up in a small West Virginia town, and while I was a late bloomer to cybersecurity, I was always fascinated by computers. As a kid, I was constantly taking things apart and putting them back together. One day, my dad brought home an old clear-case Mac from my aunt's office. I was curious, tore it down... and it never worked again. Needless to say, my dad wasn't thrilled, and growing up economically challenged with hardworking parents meant there wasn't extra money lying around for a replacement. Eventually, my mom went to college and got a computer through grant money. That’s when things blossomed for me—getting into AIM chat rooms, exploring AOL, and realizing the world behind the screen.

When it came time for college, my dad wanted me to pursue a traditional "real job." I tried Civil Engineering, but it didn't click, so I dropped out and went straight to work—doing everything from retail at Walmart to pipeline construction, before landing a job at a call center.

It wasn't until the call center that I realized I could actually make a living working with computers. An older IT guy there took me under his wing and showed me the ropes whenever he had a spare moment. With very little Pell Grant money left from my first college attempt, I needed an accelerated program that offered an actual degree alongside real industry certifications. I found West Virginia Junior College (WVJC), signed up for their accelerated Associate degree program, and pushed hard.

The hustle was real. Having a newborn right before starting school meant balancing intense financial pressure, long shifts, and late nights. My daily routine was relentless:
* **06:00 AM:** Wake up and prep for a 35-mile commute.
* **08:00 AM – 05:00 PM:** Drop off my wife at work, drop my baby at daycare, and work a full shift at the call center.
* **05:00 PM – 09:00 PM:** Pick up my baby and DoorDash with a toddler car seat in the back to cover diapers and bills.
* **10:30 PM – 01:00 AM+:** Get the family to bed, then stay up studying, labbing, and finishing coursework.

Tragically, my IT mentor passed away during my time in school, so I never got to show him what I accomplished. But when a newly graduated IT specialist joined the company needing help, I stepped up. On top of my normal duties, I helped image and pack workstations, resolved floor hardware issues, trained new hires, and supported escalation queues. 

Eventually, I got my break at **FMRS Health Systems**. They needed an "IT Guy," but I quickly realized it was far more than standard helpdesk. I learned on the job fast while finishing my degree and continuing to DoorDash while my wife worked 13-hour days. 

After graduating with Honors, I poured that same grit into engineering my own security experience: architecting production Splunk and Wazuh SIEMs, deploying GoPhish campaigns, building local MISP IOC enrichment pipelines, and triaging live threats in SentinelOne. 

Today, I’m still learning, still building, and constantly expanding my technical capabilities. If you want to connect or see what I'm building, drop me a message or check out my featured projects below!
